A New Chapter in International Tax Planning

Understanding Türkiye's 20-Year Foreign-Source Income Tax Exemption

Türkiye has introduced a tax incentive for qualifying individuals who establish tax residency in the country.

Under Repeated Article 20/D (mükerrer 20/D) of the Turkish Income Tax Law, eligible foreign-source income may benefit from an exemption lasting up to twenty years.

For internationally mobile entrepreneurs, investors and professionals, the new framework raises important questions about tax residency, income classification and cross-border compliance.

Türkiye'ye yerleşen ve gerekli şartları sağlayan gerçek kişiler için yurt dışı kaynaklı belirli kazanç ve iratlara yönelik 20 yıllık gelir vergisi istisnası düzenlenmiştir.

Bu düzenleme, uluslararası faaliyetleri bulunan yatırımcılar ve girişimciler açısından önemli vergi planlaması fırsatları sunarken gelir kaynağının doğru belirlenmesini de gerekli kılmaktadır.

02 · WHO CAN QUALIFY?

Eligibility Begins with Tax Residency

The exemption is intended for individuals who satisfy the statutory conditions and establish the required tax residency status in Türkiye.

Eligibility is not determined solely by nationality. Foreign citizens and Turkish citizens may potentially qualify, provided that all applicable requirements are fulfilled.

The framework applies to individuals regarded as settled in Türkiye from 1 January 2026.

The assessment should consider the individual's prior residency history, current circumstances and planned relocation.

03 · THE THREE-YEAR LOOK-BACK

Your Previous Tax History Matters

Before relocating to Türkiye, individuals should review their residence and tax liability status during the preceding three calendar years.

The relevant look-back requirements must be evaluated against the individual's actual circumstances, including any previous residence, registration or tax obligations in Türkiye.

Prior Turkish liability solely for rental income, investment income or capital gains does not itself prevent eligibility under the statutory exception.

04 · RELOCATING TO TÜRKİYE

Residence Is More Than a Change of Address

Relocating to Türkiye may affect an individual's worldwide tax position.

The duration of physical presence, residential arrangements, personal and economic ties, and applicable treaty provisions may influence the determination of tax residency.

Where an individual maintains connections with another country, dual-residency questions may arise.

05 · APPLICATION AND DOCUMENTATION

Turning Eligibility into Compliance

Individuals considering the exemption should establish a documented application strategy.

The process may require verification of previous tax residency, identification of qualifying income streams, preparation of supporting documents and completion of the relevant tax-office procedures.

Communiqué No. 333 requires an application to the competent tax office and an exemption certificate by the end of the settlement year; for settlement in November or December, the deadline is the end of February of the following year. Confirm the supporting documents with the tax office in advance.

06 · WHICH INCOME IS COVERED?

Foreign-Source Income vs. Türkiye-Source Income

The central distinction is the source of the income.

Potentially relevant foreign-source income categories may include:

  • Income from qualifying overseas investments
  • Dividends and interest from foreign sources
  • Rental income from overseas properties
  • Business or professional income genuinely earned outside Türkiye, subject to a source analysis

However, income received from abroad is not necessarily foreign-source income for Turkish tax purposes.

07 · THE TWENTY-YEAR FRAMEWORK

Long-Term Tax Planning Opportunities

The exemption framework provides a potentially significant planning horizon for eligible individuals.

However, the twenty-year period should not be interpreted as a blanket exemption covering every form of income.

The individual must satisfy the relevant conditions, and the income itself must fall within the statutory scope.

Qualifying exempt income is excluded from the annual return. Related costs cannot reduce taxable income, and foreign tax on that exempt income cannot be credited against Turkish income tax.

08 · CROSS-BORDER SERVICES: A CRITICAL DISTINCTION

Foreign Clients Do Not Always Mean Foreign-Source Income

Consider an individual who relocates from Germany to Türkiye and continues providing sales and marketing services to customers located outside Türkiye.

The customers are abroad, but the individual performs the services while physically present in Türkiye.

Can the resulting income qualify for the twenty-year exemption?

The answer requires an examination of the actual services, the location where activities are performed, the contractual structure and the applicable Turkish tax rules.

09 · TÜRKİYE–GERMANY TAX TREATY

The Interaction Between Domestic Exemptions and Tax Treaties

An exemption available under Turkish domestic legislation does not automatically eliminate tax obligations in another country.

For an individual relocating from Germany to Türkiye, the Türkiye–Germany Double Taxation Agreement may be relevant to:

  • Determining treaty residence
  • Allocating taxing rights between the two countries
  • Assessing business profits and other income categories
  • Addressing potential double taxation

The analysis must also consider the individual's continuing connections with Germany and the applicable German domestic tax rules.

10 · TAX COMPLIANCE AND RISK MANAGEMENT

An Exemption Requires Careful Implementation

Incorrect income classification, incomplete documentation or failure to satisfy the statutory conditions may create additional tax exposure.

Potential consequences can include tax assessments, penalties and late-payment interest, depending on the circumstances.

Individuals should retain adequate records supporting their residence status, income classification and exemption eligibility.

11 · PRACTICAL SCENARIOS

Three Situations, Different Tax Outcomes

Scenario A — Rental Income from Germany

An individual relocates to Türkiye but continues receiving rent from property located in Germany.

The income's treatment should be evaluated under the Turkish exemption rules, the Türkiye–Germany tax treaty and applicable German tax law.

Scenario B — Rental Income from İstanbul

An individual receives rent from a property situated in Türkiye.

This represents a different source-of-income analysis and should not be assumed to qualify for an exemption designed for foreign-source income.

Scenario C — Services to International Clients

An individual resides in Türkiye and provides marketing and sales services to customers abroad.

The location of the customers alone does not resolve the source-of-income question. The actual activities and applicable tax provisions must be analysed.
